Introduction

A Letter From the Governing Body

Dear Brothers and Sisters:

Love for God and for people motivates us to “go . . . and make disciples of people of all the nations, baptizing them.” ( ; ) Unselfish love is powerful. It can touch the hearts of those who are “rightly disposed for everlasting life.”— .

In the past, we focused on memorizing presentations and on leaving literature. Now we need to work on improving our conversation skills. We want to show love for others by talking about subjects that interest them. That means being flexible and carefully considering each person’s concerns and interests.

How This Brochure Works:

The brochure has 12 lessons featuring qualities that we need to cultivate in order to show love and make disciples. Each lesson is based on a Bible account in which Jesus or another first-century evangelizer displayed a specific quality in the ministry. The goal is, not to memorize presentations, but to look for ways to demonstrate our love for people.

As you consider each lesson, think carefully about how you can demonstrate the quality when speaking to people in your community. Strive to deepen your love for Jehovah and for people. That love, more than any specific technique, will help you reach your goal of making disciples.

We appreciate the great privilege to work shoulder to shoulder with you. ( ) May Jehovah richly bless you as you continue to love people and make disciples!

Your brothers,
Governing Body of Jehovah’s Witnesses

Appendix B

Should You End the Conversation?

✅ When to Continue

When someone raises a sincere question or concern, we are happy to continue the conversation. We want to speak with people who may be “rightly disposed for everlasting life.”— .

🛑 When to End Calmly & Tactfully

What if a person is angry, wants to argue, or does not want to talk right now? Calmly and tactfully end the conversation. ( ) Try to leave on good terms so that the person may be willing to converse with us in the future.— .

Key Scripture Principle (NWT): “Beginning a fight is like opening a floodgate; before the quarrel breaks out, take your leave.”—Proverbs 17:14.
